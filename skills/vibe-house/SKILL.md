# Choosing a look

The page has already done the arithmetic: it extracted the dominant colours,
it can compute contrast, and it can rotate a hue. What it cannot do is decide.
So decide, and say why in one sentence somebody could disagree with.

**A palette is usable before it is pretty.** Five colours, ordered background
first to accent last. Body text sits at 4.5:1 against the background or the
palette is wrong, however good it looks in a swatch. Large display text may
sit at 3:1. If the image's own colours cannot carry that, move them --
darkening a background is not a betrayal of the photograph.

**Move in oklch, not in RGB.** Lightness that reads as even *is* even. A
palette built by nudging hex values goes muddy in the midtones and nobody can
say why.

**Two families, and they must disagree.** A pairing works when the heading and
the body differ in structure -- a grotesque against a serif, a geometric
against a humanist. Two fonts that are nearly the same read as a mistake. Real
Google Fonts only, and ones that ship the weights being asked for.

Pairings that hold up: Fraunces with Inter · Playfair Display with Source Sans
3 · Space Grotesk with IBM Plex Sans · DM Serif Display with DM Sans ·
Instrument Serif with Geist · Libre Baskerville with Karla.

**Say what the mood is in two or three words**, lowercase, and let the palette
argue for it. "warm, worn, unhurried" tells somebody what to expect. "vibrant
and modern" tells them nothing and could describe anything.

**Iconography follows the type.** A geometric heading wants stroked icons of
even weight; a serif wants something with a bit of contrast. Do not mix icon
sets in one page.

**Do not flatter.** "why" is one sentence explaining the choice. If the image
is muddy and the honest answer is that it gives a weak palette, say so and
show what you moved.
